Strategy & Test Tips
What Jeopardy! contestants and researchers have found most useful.

Answer Format

Always phrase your response as a question: "What is...?" / "Who is...?" On the Anytime test, read carefully — some questions award partial credit for the right name even without the question framing, but always practice the format to build the habit.

High-Value Categories

  • U.S. & World Geography
  • American & World History
  • Science (especially biology & astronomy)
  • Classic Literature & Authors
  • Word origins / Potpourri
  • Pop Culture (especially 80s–2000s)

Reading Clues Quickly

Look for the category name first — it often tells you the answer's domain or format. Key signifier words like "this element," "this author," or "this capital" clue the answer type. Read to the end before locking in.

The Anytime Test

The test is 50 questions, untimed per question, over a rolling window. Strong performance usually means ~35+ correct. The test draws heavily from classic Jeopardy! categories and avoids very obscure niche topics. Speed matters — practice recalling quickly.

Building Knowledge Fast

  • J! Archive (j-archive.com) — searchable real game clues
  • Wikipedia Featured Articles — excellent breadth
  • U.S. state capitals & world capitals
  • Nobel Prize winners (especially Literature & Peace)
  • Periodic table of elements (first 30 by symbol)

Common Traps

  • Don't confuse "first" with "most famous" — clues often ask for the first person to do something
  • Author clues: know both the author AND major works
  • "Biblical" clues may be from the Old or New Testament
  • Double Jeopardy! categories often have a twist in their theme
